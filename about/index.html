METHODOLOGY AND DATA HANDLING

A useful first pass, with its limits visible.

AdScanVideo turns a short video into structured observations. It combines selected frames with dialogue when a usable transcript is available. The report is designed for creative research, production planning, notes, and review. It is not a forensic conclusion or a substitute for expert verification.

What happens during an analysis

  1. The input is validatedUploads must use a supported video format and stay under 200 MB. Public links are checked before the server retrieves them. Videos are limited to 10 minutes.
  2. A temporary workspace is createdThe server processes the video in an isolated temporary directory. The original filename is not used as a server path.
  3. Audio and captions are checkedAvailable captions are parsed and cleaned. Short uploads with usable audio may be transcribed. If no reliable transcript is available, the model is explicitly instructed not to invent speech.
  4. Frames are sampled and deduplicatedThe pipeline samples the sequence, removes near-duplicate frames, and keeps up to 20 representative images for analysis.
  5. The chosen mode shapes the reportShot breakdown, Ad intelligence, Podcast notes, Marketing copy, Tutorial steps, and Summary each use different instructions while receiving the same visual and transcript evidence.
  6. Temporary media is deletedThe uploaded video, extracted audio, captions, and frames are removed when the job finishes, fails, or times out. They are not kept as a customer video library.

What is retained

The service retains the generated analysis text and limited operational metadata needed to deliver results, enforce the daily allowance, diagnose reliability, and understand processing cost. Operational records can include job status, timestamps, mode, duration, selected-frame count, transcript availability, and cost. Analytics events exclude filenames, video URLs, report text, contact details, and job identifiers.

What the report can get wrong

  • Speaker names are unknown unless the video supplies them.
  • Speech recognition can miss words, accents, names, or technical terms.
  • Camera movement and intent are inferred from selected frames.
  • Fast cuts can compress several visual changes into one time range.
  • Legal, medical, financial, and factual claims require independent verification.

How to review a result

Use timestamps to return to the original footage. Verify quotations against the audio, confirm names and numbers, and treat creative recommendations as a starting point. For production work, add the lens, lighting, safety, ownership, and scheduling decisions that pixels alone cannot reveal.
