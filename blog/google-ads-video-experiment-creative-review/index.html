Google Ads video experiments: review the creative before you test

A video experiment can compare campaign outcomes. A footage review can help you describe what changed between the videos. Keeping those jobs separate makes the test easier to interpret.

Published October 25, 2026 · 9 min read

Before you launch a YouTube ad test, write down the decision you need the result to support. “Which version gets more views?” is useful only if view rate is the right measure for the campaign. “Does naming the benefit in the opening change qualified conversions?” is more specific, but it also depends on conversion tracking being configured and the experiment being eligible.

Google Ads currently describes a basic A/B test for video assets in eligible video reach and video view campaigns, and a custom video experiment for other supported setups. The basic path treats the creative as the variable. The platform's setup options and eligible campaign types can change, so confirm the current controls in your account before launch. Read Google's current video experiment instructions first.

Keep the evidence lanes separate.

A creative review records observations in the source video. The experiment records delivery and campaign outcomes. A compelling-looking opening is a hypothesis to test, not proof that an ad performs better.

1. Choose the experiment path and one decision

For a basic video asset A/B test, Google Ads asks you to select a success metric and an eligible control campaign, then duplicate it as the treatment arm and change the video assets. For a custom experiment, you select a control and treatment campaigns and configure the test. The current Google guide recommends one variable for a clean custom experiment. Campaign eligibility and metric options differ by experiment type.

Write the decision in one sentence before you edit:

Question: For this acquisition campaign, does an opening that states the customer problem immediately produce a higher conversion rate than the current opening?

Decision: If the test reaches the platform's stated completion threshold and tracking is valid, use the result to decide which opening to keep for this campaign and audience.

That statement identifies a creative difference, audience context and intended business outcome. It does not promise the new version will win. If conversion tracking is not ready, do not choose conversion rate and hope the platform can infer it. Choose an eligible metric that matches what you can reliably measure, or fix measurement first.

2. Review the source videos and describe the actual difference

Watch both source files with sound. Record their versions, durations, aspect ratios, first spoken line, first visible promise, first supporting proof, offer and call to action. Then replay the opening and note what is confirmed, unclear or not reviewed. Do not infer audible beats from a still frame or treat report sections as exact edit boundaries.

If the test asks about the opening, keep the rest of the creative as comparable as practical: offer, proof, CTA, duration, audio, captions and destination. If the versions also differ in creator, music and landing page, the test may still compare two complete ads, but it will not isolate the opening as the likely explanation.

Use the evidence-led hook review to label what you can verify. For a broader one-variable plan and a free worksheet, use the video ad creative testing matrix.

3. Select the success metric before the experiment runs

Google's basic video asset test lists options including view rate, click-through rate, conversion rate, cost per conversion, cost per view and other metrics. Choose a primary metric that fits the campaign objective and the next decision. Conversion metrics require valid conversion tracking in the relevant setup. Do not select a metric just because it is easy to increase.

  • Awareness or view objective: decide whether view rate, completed views or a brand measure is the outcome you need.
  • Traffic objective: define whether a click is enough, and inspect the landing page response as a separate quality check.
  • Acquisition objective: use a conversion measure only after confirming the conversion action, attribution and tag are working.

Also write down one guardrail. For example, a higher click-through rate is less useful if qualified landing-page actions fall. Your guardrail is a check on the interpretation, not a second primary success metric chosen after seeing the data.

4. Configure the test and leave the campaign conditions stable

For the eligible basic asset test, Google Ads duplicates the control campaign as the treatment and lets you add or remove video assets. Review the audience, bids, ad formats, dates and budget before saving. Google's guide says the video experiment splits traffic evenly, while its auction system can still produce differences in exposure. Check actual impressions and spend in the running test rather than assuming they are identical.

For a custom experiment, Google recommends one variable to keep the comparison clean. Avoid making unrelated campaign edits while the test is running. If delivery is very different between arms, tracking changes, ads are rejected or the campaign is learning, document that before making a creative conclusion. Follow the status and interpretation guidance displayed in your account.

5. Wait for a result you can defend

Do not stop as soon as one arm briefly leads. Google describes earlier confidence readings as directional and recommends waiting for completion for conclusive results. Check the experiment's own status and threshold, compare the selected metric over the same dates, and preserve the audience, campaign type and test setup in your notes.

A useful result statement names the scope: “In this video-view campaign, for this audience and date range, variant B had a higher reported view rate at the experiment's completed status.” A weak statement is “This is the best hook.” The first describes a measured outcome in context. The second turns one campaign result into a universal claim.

What a video analysis can and cannot tell you

A video analysis helps a reviewer organize source evidence: visible promise, product proof, sequence, dialogue, pacing observations and the CTA when those details are present in the report. Replaying the original file remains important for exact wording, sound and edit boundaries. Google's own Creative guidance in Video Analytics surfaces platform-specific creative attributes and suggestions alongside its analytics.

Use a report to form a clear question and document the differences between variants. It cannot supply your Google Ads audience, spend, conversions, lift or experiment winner. Those claims must come from the campaign data and the experiment's measurement method.

Google Ads video experiment checklist

  1. Confirm the current experiment type and eligible campaign in Google Ads.
  2. Write one hypothesis and select the success metric before launch.
  3. Review both source videos with sound and record the actual differences.
  4. Keep other creative and campaign conditions comparable when the hypothesis needs an isolated test.
  5. Verify conversion tracking if the primary metric depends on conversions.
  6. Record the experiment dates, audience, campaign type, budget and source versions.
  7. Wait for the experiment's completion status before describing a result as conclusive.
  8. Report the result with its metric, scope and limitations. Do not generalize it beyond the test.

Frequently asked questions

Can I test two different video ads in Google Ads?

Yes, where the campaign and experiment type are eligible. Google's basic A/B test for video assets supports video reach and video view campaigns; custom video experiments cover other supported setups. Confirm the current availability in your account.

Should I test a different hook or a completely new ad?

It depends on the decision. A hook-only hypothesis needs versions that keep the rest comparable. Comparing two complete ads can answer which asset performed better in that setup, but it cannot identify which individual difference caused the result.

Does a 50/50 experiment guarantee equal impressions?

No. Google describes an even traffic split, but auctions can create differences in exposure or spend. Review the live delivery data and follow the experiment's current guidance.

Can an AI report predict the winning variant?

No. It can help you document video evidence and form a hypothesis. The experiment outcome must come from the ad platform's measured campaign data.
