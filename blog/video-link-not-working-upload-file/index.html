Video link not working? Prepare a file for analysis

A link that plays in your browser is not always a video file an analyzer can retrieve. Here is a quick way to tell whether to retry the link, choose a shorter clip, or upload an authorized copy instead.

October 1, 2026 · 7 min read

Fast path: If you already have the original MP4, MOV, WebM, or MKV, use Upload a file. AdScanVideo accepts files up to 200 MB and videos up to 10 minutes. If you have only a link, check access and duration first. A failed analysis restores your free daily try.

Why does a playable URL fail in an analyzer?

Your browser may be signed in, may have a temporary playback session, or may be viewing a page rather than a directly downloadable media file. The analyzer retrieves a public video from its server; it does not inherit your browser login or permission to a private file. A platform may also limit automated retrieval even when an ordinary viewer can watch. These are possible causes, not a diagnosis of every failed URL.

For example, YouTube says private videos require an invited, signed-in viewer; age-restricted videos may not play on third-party sites. A Google Drive share link can point to a page with permissions instead of a public media file, and Drive owners can disable downloading. Do not change a private video to public just to run an analysis if it should remain private.

Use this five-minute triage

1. Check that the link is complete. Paste the full address beginning with https://. Open it in a private browser window while signed out. If the platform asks for login, permission, or an age check, a server-side analyzer may not be able to retrieve it. A successful private-window playback still does not guarantee automated access.
2. Check the clip length. AdScanVideo analyzes videos up to 10 minutes. A link to a longer recording or live stream needs an authorized clip of 10 minutes or less. Choose the segment that contains the question you want answered, then preserve enough context before and after it.
3. Identify which stage failed. “Could not be downloaded” or “platform did not provide a downloadable video” points toward link retrieval. “Video too long” points toward duration. A file-format or decoding error is different: export a standard MP4 or try another original file. If the analyzer produced a visual-only report, the video itself worked; speech or captions were unavailable or unusable.
4. Choose an authorized file. If this is your YouTube upload, YouTube Studio offers a download for videos you uploaded. If a teammate owns the source, ask for the original export or a permission-cleared clip. YouTube’s help page explicitly distinguishes your own uploads from other people’s videos; an offline Premium copy is not a general-purpose video file for uploading to another service.
5. Upload, analyze, and verify. In AdScanVideo, select Upload a file, choose MP4, MOV, WebM, or MKV under 200 MB, select the right mode, and start. When the report is ready, verify important dialogue and timestamps against the original. If it still fails, note the stage and contact support without sending a private video unless you choose to share it.

A practical example: a 35-minute interview

Illustrative example, not a customer job. An editor pastes a link to a 35-minute private interview and asks for a shot list of the opening. The link is unsuitable for two independent reasons: the analyzer cannot use the editor’s signed-in access, and the full video exceeds 10 minutes.

The editor asks the owner for the original export, trims an authorized 0:00–8:30 segment with the opening question and first answer, and uploads that clip. A shot report can now cover that segment. It cannot represent the remaining 26 minutes, so the editor labels the report “opening segment” and checks any quoted dialogue against the source.

Keep the clip focused. If you are comparing two moments far apart, make separate authorized clips and record which source and time range each represents. Do not treat two reports as one continuous transcript.

What AdScanVideo can and cannot recover

AdScanVideo samples up to 20 selected frames. It adds dialogue when usable captions or audio transcription are available, but it will not invent dialogue when they are not. An upload can solve a retrieval problem; it does not guarantee transcription quality if the source has overlapping speakers, loud music, or no speech. Common questions

Why does a public video still fail?

Public playback and server download are different permissions and technical paths. Platforms may restrict retrieval, remove formats, rate-limit requests, or change delivery behavior. Uploading an original file you have permission to use is usually the clearest fallback.

Does a failed link use my free try?

AdScanVideo restores the daily free allowance for failed jobs. If the interface does not show your restored try after a failure, refresh the page and check again; contact support if it remains missing.

Can I paste a YouTube playlist or live-stream link?

Use a single finished video or a short file clip. The product limit is 10 minutes, and a live stream is not a fixed clip ready for analysis.

Can I upload someone else’s video?

Upload only content you have the right to use. Ask the owner for the original file or permission-cleared segment rather than trying to bypass a platform’s access controls.
