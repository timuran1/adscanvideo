Match your product demo’s promise to visible proof

A cursor moves. A dashboard appears. A headline promises a result. Does the recording actually show that result? Use this product demo video audit checklist to turn vague feedback into a specific, reviewable edit brief.

What should a product demo audit answer?

For a creative strategist or small agency, the useful output is an edit decision the producer can find and understand. “Make the demo stronger” does not identify a shot. “The narration says sent, but the recording ends on a draft; show the sent confirmation or change the line” does.

Define the audience, placement and one task before reviewing. A homepage visitor deciding whether to try a product needs different detail from an existing customer learning an advanced setting. Record the exact source filename and version. An observation from yesterday’s cut may no longer apply to today’s export.

Wistia’s product-page video guidance distinguishes explaining a product’s purpose from demonstrating its operation. That distinction makes a useful review question: does this scene explain a feature, show someone using it, or show the outcome? A polished interface shot can do the first without doing the other two.

1. Replay the first three seconds

Watch the opening at normal speed, first with its intended audio and then muted. Do not infer a spoken promise from a silent still. Write four short observations:

  • Opening promise: copy the visible wording and separately note any spoken line you actually heard.
  • Visible proof: identify the product state or action present in the image. A logo establishes identity; it does not establish the claimed outcome.
  • Transition: describe how the opening leads into the demonstration. Record approximate ranges for navigation, then replay to check actual cuts.
  • Audible beat: note a relevant spoken cue or sound only after listening. If sound was not reviewed, leave it Not reviewed.

These are human review fields. AdScanVideo may provide useful candidate observations, but this checklist does not imply that the product generates a numeric Hook Scorecard. See the first-three-seconds hook review for a broader opening worksheet.

2. Separate action from outcome

Write what changed before and after the action. “Clicks Export” describes an action. “A downloaded file opens with the expected content” describes a visible outcome. If the clip cuts away after the click, the outcome remains unconfirmed from this recording.

Check continuity too. Does the result belong to the same input? Does a time jump conceal a required step? A shortened workflow can be appropriate, but an edit should not imply that a slow or conditional operation happened instantly. If timing matters to the promise, preserve a continuous reference or document how the duration was measured.

Promise

What is being claimed? Record exact on-screen wording and separately verified speech.

Action

What does the user do? Name the visible input and operation, without filling in hidden steps.

Outcome

What result can a viewer inspect? Locate the actual confirmation, generated artifact or changed state.

Evidence gap

What is still unknown? Keep external performance, hidden operations and unreviewed audio separate.

Next step

What can the viewer do now? Check the CTA wording, destination and current offer independently.

A practical review row would say: Visible action checked; sending outcome Unclear. Proposed edit: show the actual sent confirmation, or change the promise to creating a draft. Timing claim: Not reviewed until measured. This does not say the product cannot send invoices. It says this cut does not establish it.

Make the hypothesis testable: “Will showing the confirmation help intended viewers correctly identify the completed task?” Ask a few representative reviewers what happened before explaining it. Record their answers and the tested version. Comprehension feedback is useful, but it is not a measured conversion lift.

4. Use decisions instead of an unexplained score

For each check, choose Confirmed when the reviewed evidence answers the specific question, Unclear when you reviewed it but the evidence does not settle it, or Not reviewed when the check has not happened. A blank cell is not approval.

Then choose a separate action: Keep, Revise or Obtain evidence. For example, a clearly visible unsupported headline can be Confirmed as wording while its promised business result remains Unclear. Do not collapse those into one reassuring number. This rubric is editorial and belongs to the worksheet; it is not an automated product rating.

5. Use AI analysis to find places to inspect

  1. Upload the permitted source file and choose Ad intelligence for an ad-oriented first pass. Choose Shot breakdown when you need visual scene notes.
  2. Read the actual returned report. Keep its wording and time reference in a separate field from your own replay notes.
  3. Replay important ranges in the original with context on either side. Native video analysis can miss or misinterpret brief actions and audio; timestamps are navigation aids, not frame-exact edit instructions.
  4. Write the evidence gap and an editorial edit hypothesis. Do not present your hypothesis as generated output.
  5. Review the revised export and record whether the requested change is visible. Leave campaign results unmeasured until there is appropriate performance data.

Frequently asked questions

How long should a product demo be?

Long enough to answer its defined buyer question. Show a complete, relevant task, then remove detail that does not help that audience. There is no universal length that guarantees engagement or sales.

Can AI verify the product’s performance claims?

A video report can describe visible actions and available speech. It cannot independently establish revenue, time savings across users or campaign performance from a clip alone. Check those claims against their own evidence.

What if I cannot hear the original audio?

Mark the audio check Not reviewed. Do not treat subtitles, a silent preview or an AI summary as independent confirmation of the spoken wording. Obtain the source with sound and replay it before making an audio claim.
