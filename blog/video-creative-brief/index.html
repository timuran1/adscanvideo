How to write a video creative brief from a reference ad

A reference ad is useful when it gives your team a shared target. A creative brief explains the decisions behind that target so you can make an original piece with the same strategic clarity.

September 20, 2026 · 6 min read

Begin with the audience and the job to be done

Before describing colors or camera moves, state who the ad is for and what should change after they watch it. “People who need faster weekly reporting” is more useful than “busy professionals.” The job might be to understand a problem, believe a claim, or take a specific next step.

Brief essentials

  • Audience: who is most likely to recognize the problem?
  • Promise: what valuable outcome does the product offer?
  • Proof: what evidence makes the promise believable?
  • Objection: what concern could stop the viewer?
  • Action: what should the viewer do next?

Reverse-engineer the reference ad's structure

Write down what happens in order, then label the job each beat performs. A strong short ad often opens with a recognizable tension, makes the product visible, gives one concrete proof point, and closes with a clear action. The exact shots can change; the communication sequence is what your team needs to understand.

00:00–00:02
Pattern interrupt

Show the problem before the viewer has time to scroll.

00:02–00:08
Demonstration

Make the product's useful action visible, not merely described.

00:08–00:15
Proof and CTA

Give one reason to believe, then make the next step unmistakable.

Describe tone as behavior

“Make it premium” is hard to execute. Use observable choices: restrained editing, clean backgrounds, direct eye contact, a calm voiceover, and one accent color. Separate what the reference proves from what you are inferring. This keeps the brief confident without copying another brand's surface style.

Keep one message in the center

A brief becomes less useful every time it adds another audience, benefit, or CTA. Choose one primary promise. Supporting details should make that promise easier to believe, not compete with it. If the ad needs several products or offers, make the hierarchy explicit before production starts.

Use AI for observation, judgment for strategy

AI video analysis can quickly identify hooks, scenes, pacing, dialogue, on-screen text, product moments, and calls to action. It cannot decide whether a claim is legally approved, whether the audience will trust it, or what your brand should stand for. Use the analysis as evidence for the brief, then let your strategist and creative team make the final choices.
