Video Ad Hook Checklist: Review the First 3 Seconds

Ad Intelligence · Updated October 11, 2026 · 6 min read

A useful hook review begins with what a viewer can actually see and hear. Write down the opening promise, the evidence supporting it and the point where the message becomes clear. Then choose one edit to test. Calling an opening compelling is an opinion; recording its words, images and sequence gives your editor something to work with.

This guide gives you a manual Hook Scorecard for the first three seconds, a concrete visual example and a downloadable worksheet. It does not predict retention, sales or whether an ad is a winner. Those require campaign evidence.

Why check the first three seconds?

TikTok's creative guidance recommends introducing the content proposition within the first three seconds and prioritizing the hook within the first six. It also describes its recommendations as starting points for testing. Three seconds is a useful review window, not a universal deadline for every format or audience.

Google's ABCD guidance connects attention, branding, connection and direction. It recommends focused messaging, purposeful pacing and a clear call to action, with audio and text reinforcing the story. Apply the relevant platform guidance to your placement rather than assuming one opening will work everywhere.

The Hook Scorecard: five checks with evidence

For each check, select Confirmed, Unclear or Not reviewed. Confirmed means you can point to source evidence answering the question. Unclear means you reviewed the relevant material but the answer remains ambiguous. Not reviewed means you have not checked it. These are editorial review labels, not product generated scores or pass/fail performance ratings.

CheckQuestionEvidence to record
Opening promiseWhat does the opening offer this viewer?Exact visible text or verified spoken wording. If no promise is explicit, record that.
Visible proofWhat supports the promise on screen?A demonstration, product detail or visible outcome. A prop alone does not establish what is being sold.
Transitions and pacingCan the message be understood before the next change?Replayed cuts, overlay changes and approximate ranges. Count changes you verified, not inferred cuts from report sections.
Audible beatsDo speech, music or effects reinforce the opening?Listen to the original audio. Record heard words and relevant changes. A silent screenshot cannot answer this.
Offer continuityDoes the opening connect to the eventual offer and next step?Review the rest of the ad and its landing page. This check extends beyond the opening window.

Do not add these labels into a numerical retention score. A clear opening can still perform poorly, and an unusual opening may work for a specific audience. The scorecard helps you document a review and form a testable hypothesis.

Example: a visible object is not automatically the offer

In this still, a person and a blue chair are visible. That is a manual visual observation. It does not establish that the chair is the advertised product, that a benefit has been demonstrated or that viewers will keep watching.

A safe review entry is: Visible proof: Unclear. The chair is visible, but the offer cannot be established from this still. Replay the opening with sound and check the later offer. Audio and transitions remain Not reviewed when working from the still alone.

An edit hypothesis could be: If the intended offer is a chair, test a version that identifies the chair and its relevant benefit earlier. That is a conditional suggestion. It is not a finding that the current clip has a pacing defect or proof that the edit will improve conversions.

Keep three things separate: what the generated report says, what you verified in the original source and what you propose changing.

Turn the review into one controlled creative test

Pick the most important unresolved message, then change one element that could clarify it. For example, move an existing benefit caption earlier while keeping the offer, audience and destination comparable. Record the version and the hypothesis before launching.

Use your platform's retention and campaign results to judge that test. Then follow the visitors through to qualified site engagement, analysis starts, completed reports and verified payments. A rise in views alone does not establish that the hook attracted the right people. Report the audience, placement, dates and sample size alongside any outcome.

Frequently asked questions

Does AdScanVideo produce this Hook Scorecard automatically?

This scorecard is an editorial worksheet. Use the actual generated report as one input and verify it in the source. Do not present the worksheet labels as AI generated product scores.

Can I call a competitor ad a winning hook?

Only if you have credible performance evidence and explain what winning means. Views, visibility or repeated appearances do not establish spend, profitability or conversion performance.

Should I remove everything that appears after three seconds?

No. Review the opening in context. A slower introduction might fit the story or placement. The goal is to understand the promise and test an appropriate change, not apply a fixed cut to every ad.

Can a report prove the exact moment a cut happens?

Verify editing boundaries against the original footage. Report timestamps can guide navigation, but they are not a guarantee of frame exact cut detection.
