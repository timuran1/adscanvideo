Turn a reference shot into an AI video prompt

AI Video · Updated October 3, 2026 · 7 min read

Start with what the reference actually shows. Then make the creative choices for your new shot. A video analyzer can draft the first part; it cannot decide your story, guarantee an exact recreation, or see every frame.

Quick workflow: Pick one short moment → record the visible subject, action, frame, light, and motion → mark uncertain details → write a new prompt → generate, compare, and revise.

Separate observation from invention

“Two characters in a field” identifies subjects. A production prompt also says what moves, how the camera behaves, where the action ends, and which details matter. A shot report gives you a first pass at those observations, but you should check the original around each timestamp.

Do not guess a precise lens focal length from a sampled frame. When a camera appears to move, describe the visible effect, such as “a subtle push in,” rather than claiming the original used a dolly or a digital zoom. Brief actions can fall between sampled frames.

A real sample, followed by a new creative prompt

Our published eight-second sample opens with an animated orange cat and a small duck in a grassy field beneath a blue sky. The report describes a slight zoom around 00:00–00:01. That is a reported observation from processed footage, not a generated video prompt.

Now retain the useful direction—a readable two-subject composition and restrained camera push—but choose original characters and setting:

Human-written adaptation, not analyzer output: “Vertical animated medium shot. Two original paper-cutout characters stand together in a sunlit rooftop garden. Begin with both figures clearly visible. The camera pushes in gently while leaves move in a light breeze. Bright sky, simple silhouettes, playful colors. Hold both figures in frame at the end.”

The first description reports the reference. The second is a brief for a different scene. Keep those artifacts separate when the reference includes someone else’s characters, artwork, brand, or product claim.

Copyable video-to-prompt worksheet

  • Subject: Who or what is visible? Avoid guessing identity from one frame.
  • Action: What single, continuous action should happen?
  • Frame: What shot size, viewpoint, placement, and aspect ratio do you want?
  • Motion: How do the subject, camera, and background move?
  • Look: Which lighting, palette, and setting details are genuinely visible?
  • End state: Where should the subject be when the shot finishes?

Template: [format and shot size] + [subject and setting] + [one action] + [camera behavior] + [lighting and style] + [end state].

Begin with the essential physical action and add detail only when it helps. Google’s Veo guide recommends clear subject, action, style, camera, composition, and ambiance; Runway recommends simple starting prompts and iteration.

Adapt the same observation to your generator

Text-to-video

Describe the whole new scene: subject, setting, one action, camera behavior, and look. Set 9:16 or 16:9 in the generator controls where available. Test a clear motion before adding dialogue, on-screen text, or several scene changes.

Image-to-video

The starting image already supplies much of the appearance and composition. Concentrate the text on motion, camera behavior, and what changes over time. This follows Runway’s image-to-video guidance. A video analysis does not create or license a starting image for you.

Several shots

Write one prompt per intentional beat, then assemble the clips in an editor. Track character appearance, wardrobe, props, travel direction, lighting, and aspect ratio across generations. Do not force a long montage into one short generation prompt.

Common questions

Can AI turn a video into a prompt?

It can draft descriptions from selected visual moments. Review the footage, then translate the description into an instruction for your new creative goal and generator.

Will it capture every shot?

No. AdScanVideo samples frames and may miss fast edits or actions. Short, focused clips make better starting points than long montages.

Can I use one prompt for Veo and Runway?

Reuse the observed shot and core idea, but adapt the inputs. Image-to-video workflows often need a starting image and motion-focused wording, while text-to-video needs the scene described in the prompt.
