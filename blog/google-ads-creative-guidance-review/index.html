Google Ads Creative Guidance: review video ads before you test

Video Analytics can surface creative suggestions for an ad. Here is how to find them, check the original video, and turn one observation into a clear experiment question.

Published October 27, 2026 · 8 min read

Google Ads Creative guidance gives advertisers ideas to consider while reviewing video ads. A suggestion is a starting point for inspection, not a forecast of campaign results. First check what the feature actually says, then decide whether the source video supports a useful change and how you would measure it.

Keep three records separate.

Save the Google Ads suggestion as platform guidance, the video review as source evidence, and the campaign result as measured performance. One does not stand in for another.

Find Creative guidance below a video's retention curves

Google's current help page places the feature under Campaigns → Assets → Videos → Analytics. Select an ad and look below its retention curves for Ideas to try. The section may not appear for every asset or account. If it is missing, record that you could not access the panel instead of assuming that Google found no issue.

Google lists suggestions concerning whether a brand logo appears in the first five seconds, whether duration suits the campaign objective, whether the video has a human voice-over, and whether an ad group contains landscape, square, and vertical video formats. Check the current Google Ads Creative guidance documentation for availability and definitions because the interface can change.

Turn each suggestion into a source check

Open the exact video asset used by the ad. Do not use a newer edit with the same name. Record the asset identifier, duration, aspect ratio, and version date, then inspect the relevant evidence in the source.

Show a brand logo within the first five secondsReplay the opening with sound and note when the logo first becomes visible. Record what appears behind or beside it.Is the brand identifiable early enough for this ad's objective without obscuring the opening proof?
Align duration with the campaign objectiveCheck the actual cut length, opening, main proof, offer, and ending. Compare alternate lengths only if they exist.Which useful information would be removed, and which campaign outcome would the shorter cut be intended to affect?
Consider human voice-overListen to the original audio. Note spoken explanation, music, pauses, and whether on-screen copy carries the message without narration.Would narration clarify a demonstrated action for this audience, or merely add another element?
Use landscape, square, and vertical formatsInspect the actual exports and preview each crop. Check where text, faces, product proof, and logos land in each version.Does each crop preserve the information needed for its placement, rather than just filling the frame?

When a field is not visible or audible in the original, write “not verified.” Do not infer a voice-over from captions or claim a logo is absent because one sampled preview cropped it out. A report can help organize observations, but replay the source before using a note in the brief. Choose one testable next step

  1. Copy the guidance verbatim. Add the date, campaign, ad and video asset so the note remains attached to the right version.
  2. State one question. For example: “For this campaign and audience, does adding a clear brand mark during the opening change the selected view-rate metric?” This is a hypothesis, not a result.
  3. List the intended edit. Identify only the planned change and note other differences such as crop, opening line, music, length, offer, or call to action.
  4. Name the measure first. Choose an available campaign metric tied to the decision and check that any conversion tracking is ready before launch.
  5. Review results in the campaign. Follow current experiment eligibility and platform guidance. A creative checklist cannot show whether the variant won.

Example record, not campaign data: Guidance: “show your logo in the first five seconds.” Source check: the supplied cut begins with a product demonstration and the logo appears later. Editorial hypothesis: test an opening version with a small, legible brand mark while preserving the product action. Measurement: select an eligible campaign metric before launch. Result: leave blank until the experiment runs and is reviewed.

Frequently asked questions

Where is Creative guidance in Google Ads Video Analytics?

Open Campaigns, then Assets, then Videos. Choose Analytics, select an ad, and look below its retention curves for Ideas to try. Interface labels and availability can vary by account.

What does Google Ads Creative guidance check?

Google lists suggestions about showing a brand logo in the first five seconds, video duration relative to the campaign objective, human voice-over, and using landscape, square, and vertical formats in an ad group.

Does Creative guidance tell me whether an ad will perform better?

No. Guidance is a set of creative suggestions. It does not establish that changing a video will improve views, conversions, or another campaign outcome. Measure the outcome in the campaign.

Should I change every suggested item at once?

If you need to learn which change mattered, write a single hypothesis and keep other important creative and campaign conditions comparable. A broad refresh can still be a valid business choice, but it cannot isolate one cause.
