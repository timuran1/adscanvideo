How to Review a Video Ad’s First 3 Seconds with Retention Data

Ad Intelligence · Updated October 30, 2026 · 7 min read

The first few seconds are worth a close review, but they are not a universal pass or fail threshold. Use the video, the placement and the campaign data together. A retention curve can show when viewers leave. It cannot, by itself, tell you why they left or whether they would have converted.

This guide is for editors and paid-media teams reviewing a YouTube video ad. It uses Google's current reporting guidance and a source-first review process. The example points below are instructions for inspecting your own campaign, not performance results from a customer or AdScanVideo test.

Start with the right performance view

In Google Ads, the Video Analytics view separates ad delivery data from organic YouTube data. Make sure the selected view, campaign, date range and format match the question you are asking. Do not blend Shorts, in-feed and in-stream results into one opening verdict without checking their different delivery and view definitions.

Google's creative guidance for app video campaigns suggests trying to capture attention within the first 2 to 3 seconds. That advice is scoped to app campaigns. Treat it as a prompt to inspect the opening, not a rule that every product, platform or audience must follow.

A five-pass opening review

  1. Watch the source without the chart. Replay from 0:00 with sound on. Write down what is visibly promised, what proof appears, when the product or problem becomes clear, and what the audio actually says. Avoid inferred benefit claims.
  2. Check sound-off comprehension separately. Mute the clip and note whether the first image or on-screen words still orient the viewer. This is a human review question, not a retention metric.
  3. Open audience retention. Google Ads describes this card as the percentage of viewers who made it to each second. Mark a noticeable dip, then replay that exact region and a little context before and after it.
  4. Check the format before interpreting a dip. Google notes that a steep drop after five seconds is common for skippable in-stream ads when the skip button appears. A drop at that point is not automatically evidence of a bad opening. Compare with the same format and objective where possible.
  5. Write one edit hypothesis. Example: “Move the product demonstration currently after the opening line into the first shot, then compare the same-format retention curve.” This is a proposed test, not a predicted lift. Record the version, audience, placement and date so the comparison stays interpretable.

Know what each metric can tell you

SignalUseful forDoes not establish
Per-second audience retentionLocating where the share of viewers still watching changesThe reason for a dip or the viewer’s intent
Video played to 25%, 50%, 75% or 100%Comparing broad completion pointsThe exact second where a viewer left
View rateViews relative to impressions under that format’s definitionMessage comprehension, persuasion or a purchase
Conversion reportingActions counted by the campaign’s configured conversion eventsThat the creative alone caused the action

Google cautions that quartile reporting is directional and may not match a manual calculation of view rate, particularly for in-feed ads. Use the report for the question it measures and keep the other metrics distinct.

Turn the curve into a careful edit test

If the retention line changes around a product reveal, replay that moment and describe what actually happens: the product enters the frame, the spoken claim begins, the shot changes, or the skip control becomes available. Then ask whether a different sequence is worth testing. Do not label a timestamped observation a cause.

Before a test, choose the outcome that matters. If the goal is qualified traffic, inspect the configured click and landing-page events alongside retention. If it is awareness, review the applicable view and reach measures. Keep edits comparable and avoid changing the audience, bid, placement and creative all at once when you want to learn which change mattered.

For the visual opening itself, use the separate video-ad hook checklist to record an editorial review. For platform suggestions, see our guide to Google Ads Creative guidance. For the next step after observing an ad, use the video ad creative testing matrix to plan a controlled variant.

Common questions

Are the first three seconds a universal rule for every video ad?

No. They are a useful opening segment to review. Google's 2 to 3 second suggestion cited above is specifically about app video ads, not a universal platform cutoff.

Does an early retention drop prove the hook is weak?

No. Check expected skip behavior, format, placement, audience and the exact content at that point. The curve shows a change in watching, not a causal explanation.

Can an AI video report explain why viewers left or whether the ad converted?

No. AdScanVideo produces timestamped video observations. Campaign retention and conversion come from their respective analytics systems. They are separate evidence sources.

How should I score the opening?

Write down a rubric first, label the score as editorial judgment, and keep it separate from generated observations and campaign outcomes. Do not present a human score as a product metric.
