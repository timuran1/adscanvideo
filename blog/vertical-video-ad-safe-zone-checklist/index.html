A safe-zone checklist for vertical video ads

Your opening line can be persuasive, yet invisible under a platform label. Your product demo can land behind the reaction buttons. Use this timestamped audit before publishing a Shorts, Reels, or TikTok creative.

September 24, 2026 · 6 min read

Start with the placement, not a generic margin. The visible area changes with platform controls, ad format, and device. Use the current platform safe-zone template and preview for the exact placement you plan to run. A text box that looks centered in an editor may still be covered in the feed.

Why a 9:16 export can still be hard to read

Vertical framing gives an ad more screen space, but the app adds interface elements on top of the video. Google’s video ad specifications show safe-zone references for YouTube and warn that overlays and calls to action vary by format, campaign type, and screen. Meta’s Reels guidance points advertisers to its own safe-zone checker. TikTok’s creative guidance likewise tells advertisers to keep important elements in its safe zone. YouTube’s own Shorts editor gives warnings when text or stickers approach areas that may be hidden.

Those sources explain why a single pixel rule is unreliable. Review each intended placement with its current official template, then look at the actual ad preview. When adapting the same footage for several placements, keep a separate pass/fail note for each one.

First map the moments a viewer must see

Before measuring placement, make a short timeline. Note the first promise, first product appearance, demonstration, on-screen claim, price or offer, and final call to action. If a moment is covered, ask whether spoken audio or another frame still communicates it. Do not assume a viewer will pause or turn sound on to recover missing information.

Opening
Hook and audience cue

Can the viewer read the key line and see the subject immediately, without a top label or caption covering them?

Middle
Product demonstration

Are the product, hands, before-and-after evidence, and labels visible when right-side controls appear?

Throughout
Captions and claims

Can each caption be read at phone size? Does any platform caption, sticker, or progress bar compete with it?

Close
Offer and next action

Is the price, offer, or CTA clear in the feed preview, above any bottom controls and with enough time to read?

A timestamped shot breakdown makes this map easier to build. It can identify where scenes and visible overlays appear, but it cannot certify every frame or simulate every app interface.

Run a placement-by-placement audit

Six checks before export

  • Choose the destination. Write down every intended platform and placement, including Shorts, Reels, or TikTok ad format. A 9:16 file is only the starting format.
  • Get the current template. Use the official safe-zone guide or transparent template for that placement. Avoid recycled diagrams with unexplained margins.
  • Scrub the entire clip. Pause at each hook, product, claim, caption, offer, and CTA. Very brief text can fall between sampled frames.
  • Preview on a phone. Use the platform’s ad preview where available. Google Ads provides a Shorts preview; other placement previews should be checked in their own tools.
  • Test sound low and on. Check whether captions and visuals still carry the essential point without audio, then verify any spoken claim against the video.
  • Move and re-export. Fix the highest-value obscured element first, export again, and repeat the same preview. Do not rely on how the editor canvas looks.

A worked example: hypothetical 20-second product ad

This is an invented audit example, not an actual ad or an AdScanVideo output. The safe-zone risks are questions to check in placement previews, not guaranteed cropping on every device.

00:00–00:03
Hook near the top

“Packing takes 30 seconds” appears above a bag. Check whether a placement header covers the line. If it does, move the hook lower or change the opening frame.

00:03–00:11
Demo on the right

A hand fastens the bag near the right edge. Check whether reaction controls hide the fastening action. Reframe the source shot if that action is the proof.

00:11–00:16
Caption and price

The price sits over a busy background near the bottom. Check both contrast and overlap with native captions or ad labels. A solid backing and repositioning may solve different problems.

00:16–00:20
CTA at the edge

“See sizes” appears only in the final second. If it is covered or too brief, extend the end card and preview again. The exact duration needed depends on the copy and placement.

Decision: Record “pass,” “revise,” or “check in preview” for each beat. Do not mark the whole ad safe because one frame looks clean. For the creative strategy itself, use the UGC ad scorecard to review hook, proof, offer, and CTA separately from layout.

Frequently asked questions

Is there one safe-zone template for all vertical video ads?

No. Interface overlays differ by platform, placement, campaign format, and device. Use the current platform template and an in-platform preview for each placement you intend to run.

Can AI video analysis certify that an ad is safe for every placement?

No. A timestamped analysis can help locate captions, product moments, and calls to action, but sampled frames may miss a brief overlay. A person should scrub the full video and check official placement previews.

What should I fix first if a vertical ad has several safe-zone problems?

Prioritize any obscured opening promise, product demonstration, price, or call to action. Then check captions, logos, and decorative elements. Re-preview each revised export.
