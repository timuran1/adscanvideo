Face swap, character swap or recreate: which AI video tool do you need?

Three different jobs get called “AI video swap”. They take different inputs, change different things, and fail in different ways. Pick the job first, then the tool.

Published November 27, 2026 · 5 min read

Start with what must stay the same. If the clip is right and one element is wrong, you want a swap. If the idea is right but the footage is not yours to use, you want to recreate it as a new video.

The three jobs

JobYou provideWhat changesWhat stays
Face swapA clip and a face photoThe face onlyBody, hair, clothes, scene, motion
Character or product swapA clip and photos of the person, product or objectThe whole person or objectScene, camera, timing of the action
Motion transferA clip and a character photoEverything except the movementThe movement and timing
RecreateA prompt, with optional photos, a clip or a voiceEverything: it is a new videoOnly the idea you describe

Six questions that decide it

  1. Is the original footage yours to use? A swap still contains the original scene. If you do not have the rights to the clip, recreate the idea with your own setting.
  2. Is it only the face, or the whole person? A face swap keeps the original body and clothes. If the wardrobe, build or hair is wrong for your brand, you need a character swap.
  3. Is the thing to replace a product? That is an object swap. Results depend on how the product is held, turned and lit, so test a short clip before you commit to a long one.
  4. How long is the clip? Most generation tools work on short clips and charge by the second. Check the maximum length and what a full-length run costs.
  5. What resolution do you need? Some swaps return a lower resolution than the source. That can be fine for a concept test and wrong for a final master.
  6. Do you have consent? Putting a real person into a video, or replacing one, needs that person’s agreement and the rights to the footage.

Other kinds of tools

We have not tested the tools below, so treat this as a map of categories, not a ranking. Check each tool’s own page for current features, limits and data handling.

  • Face-only swap apps. Many web and phone apps replace just the face. They are the right choice when body and clothes should stay.
  • Character replacement in a browser. Tools such as Magic Hour and Higgsfield are commonly listed in roundups for replacing a whole character in a clip.
  • Local, open-source face swapping. FaceFusion runs on your own machine, which suits people who do not want to upload footage and have a capable graphics card.
  • General video generators. Text-to-video models can recreate an idea from a prompt, usually without a step that analyzes your reference video first.

A short checklist before you pay for a run

  • Trim the clip to the seconds you need. You pay by the second.
  • Use a clear, well-lit reference photo that shows what the shot will show (face, upper body or the whole product).
  • Run a 4 to 5 second test before a long clip.
  • Watch the whole result for stutter, warped hands, changed logos and unreadable text.
  • Keep the original and note what was generated, so you can label it where a platform requires that.

Questions

What is the difference between a face swap and a character swap?

A face swap replaces only the face and keeps the original body, hair and clothes. A character swap replaces the whole person, including body and clothing, while keeping the scene and the movement.

Which AI tool do I need to put my product into an existing video?

You need an object or product swap, not a face swap. It takes your clip and photos of the product and replaces the object while keeping the shot. Test it on a short clip, because results vary with how the product is held and lit.

When should I recreate a video instead of swapping?

Recreate when you want a new video based on an idea, with your own setting, people and product. Swap when the existing clip is right and only one element has to change.

Do I need permission to swap a person into a video?

Yes. You need the rights to the footage and the consent of any identifiable person whose face, body or voice is used or replaced.
